Jobber vs Housecall Pro for HVAC (2026)

Jobber and Housecall Pro are the two strongest field service platforms for HVAC shops with 3–20 technicians, and most owners genuinely weighing their options land on one of these two. They overlap heavily — both do scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, payments, and mobile work orders well — so the decision comes down to a few specific differences that matter more for HVAC than for other trades: maintenance-plan automation, the client portal, Android app quality, and Canada coverage.

The Short Answer

Choose Housecall Pro if maintenance plans are central to your revenue. Its Service Plans feature auto-bills and auto-schedules recurring tune-up agreements — the single most valuable thing HVAC software can automate, and Housecall Pro does it best. Its marketing and review-request tools are also stronger.

Choose Jobber if you want the strongest client self-service portal, the best Android apps, native US + Canada coverage, or multi-trade flexibility. Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, making it the natural pick for Canadian shops, and its 14-day trial needs no card. Maintenance agreements work as recurring jobs but aren't auto-billed the way Housecall Pro's are.

Where Housecall Pro Wins for HVAC

Maintenance-plan automation. This is the decisive HVAC difference. Housecall Pro's Service Plans let you build an agreement (say two tune-ups a year), enroll a customer, and have the software automatically bill them on your chosen cadence while scheduling each visit. For a shop growing a book of recurring maintenance revenue, that automation directly compounds income. Jobber can schedule recurring visits but doesn't auto-bill the subscription, so you carry more of the billing manually.

Marketing and reviews. Housecall Pro's automated review requests, follow-up sequences, and postcard campaigns are the best in this category — valuable if your growth comes from Google reviews and repeat customers.

Built-in consumer financing. For the big-ticket install and replacement jobs that define HVAC, Housecall Pro's built-in financing options help techs close on the spot.

Where Jobber Wins for HVAC

Client self-service portal. Jobber's client hub — approve quotes, track jobs, view service history, pay online — is the strongest at this price and a real advantage for shops serving property managers and commercial accounts who want visibility without calling.

Android apps. If your technicians use Android, this matters daily: Jobber has strong parity between iOS and Android (~4.6/5 both), while Housecall Pro's Android app rates around 3.3/5.

Canada and multi-trade. Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton and is the natural choice for Canadian HVAC shops. If you run HVAC alongside plumbing or electrical, one Jobber account covers all of it. And the card-free 14-day trial lowers the risk of trying it.

Lower entry price. Jobber Core starts at $49/mo vs Housecall Pro Basic at $79/mo, and Connect ($119) undercuts Essentials ($189) at the team tier — though the plans include slightly different features, so compare on what you'll actually use.

Which Should You Pick?

Pick Housecall Pro if: maintenance/service agreements are a core revenue stream; you grow through Google reviews and repeat business; you sell financed installs and want financing built in; your techs are on iPhones.

Pick Jobber if: you want the best client portal; your team uses Android; you operate in Canada; you run multiple trades under one roof; you want a card-free trial before committing; or a lower entry price matters.

Still torn? If maintenance plans dominate your business, Housecall Pro. For everything else — and for most shops that just want the safest all-round HVAC platform — start with Jobber's free trial and test it against your real workflow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Jobber or Housecall Pro better for HVAC?

Both are excellent for HVAC shops with 3–20 techs; the right one depends on your priorities. Housecall Pro is better if maintenance/service agreements are central to your revenue — its Service Plans auto-bill and auto-schedule those agreements — and if you sell financed installs or grow through reviews. Jobber is better if you want the strongest client portal, your team uses Android, you operate in Canada, or you run multiple trades. If maintenance plans dominate, choose Housecall Pro; otherwise Jobber is the safest all-round pick and offers a card-free trial.

Which is better for HVAC maintenance agreements?

Housecall Pro, clearly. Its Service Plans feature is purpose-built for recurring maintenance: enroll a customer once and it automatically bills them (monthly, quarterly, or annually) and schedules each visit. Jobber can set up recurring maintenance visits as recurring jobs, but it doesn't auto-bill the subscription, so you do more billing manually. If your business runs on a large book of tune-up agreements, that difference alone can decide it in Housecall Pro's favor.

Which is cheaper, Jobber or Housecall Pro?

Jobber has a lower entry point — Core at $49/mo vs Housecall Pro Basic at $79/mo — and its team plan (Connect, $119/mo for up to 5) undercuts Housecall Pro Essentials ($189/mo for up to 5). But the plans bundle different features: Housecall Pro Essentials includes the Service Plans maintenance automation and review tools, while Jobber Connect includes online booking and two-way texts. Compare on the features you'll actually use rather than headline price, and note both offer ~20% off with annual billing.

Which works better in Canada?

Both operate in Canada, but Jobber has the edge — it's headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, with strong Canadian market penetration and support. Housecall Pro explicitly covers Canadian customers too. For a Canadian HVAC shop, Jobber is the more natural default, though either will work. Pricing is in USD for both; Canadian users pay the CAD equivalent.
