Quick Verdict

Housecall Pro is the strongest all-round fit for a residential HVAC shop, and the single biggest reason is maintenance plans. Its Service Plans feature is purpose-built for the HVAC recurring-revenue model: set up an agreement once — say two tune-ups a year — and the software auto-bills the customer, auto-schedules the visits, and handles the reminders. For a business trying to grow a book of recurring maintenance revenue, nothing else in this category makes that as easy.

The main catches are add-on cost creep (features that look included sometimes carry an extra charge), a weaker Android app than iOS, and phone support locked to premium tiers. If your maintenance-plan book is central to your business, those are manageable trade-offs. If your techs run Android or you need heavy reporting, test carefully during a demo.

What Housecall Pro Actually Does

Housecall Pro is a field service platform used across HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and similar trades, covering the full job lifecycle: scheduling, dispatch, estimates, invoicing, and payment collection. For HVAC businesses specifically, several features are built around how the trade actually operates — real-time GPS tracking of technicians, a drag-and-drop dispatch board, automatic "on my way" texts to customers, and built-in consumer financing for the big-ticket install and replacement jobs that define HVAC.

The standout for HVAC is Recurring Service Plans. You create a maintenance agreement — for example, a spring AC tune-up and a fall furnace check at $199/year — and Housecall Pro enrolls the customer, bills them automatically on the cadence you choose (monthly, quarterly, or annually), schedules each visit, and communicates with the customer along the way. Customers can pay by card, ACH bank transfer, or digital wallet. For an HVAC shop, this is the difference between a maintenance program you have to chase and one that runs itself.

The online booking page is polished and customizable — embed it on your website or share the link on Google and social so customers can book a tune-up or service call around the clock without a phone call.

How It Works on a Real HVAC Job

A homeowner books a fall furnace tune-up through your online booking page. The job lands on the dispatch board; the assigned tech gets it on their phone. When the tech leaves for the appointment, the customer gets an automatic "on my way" text with an ETA. On site, the tech records the work, adds any refrigerant or parts used, attaches photos, and — if the customer needs a new system — presents financing options on the spot.

Afterward, the invoice generates automatically. If the customer isn't already on a maintenance plan, the tech or office can enroll them in one: two visits a year, auto-billed. From then on, Housecall Pro schedules each seasonal visit and bills the plan without anyone remembering to. That is the recurring-revenue engine that makes maintenance plans worth building — and Housecall Pro automates the part most shops let slip.

The Catch

Add-on cost creep is the most consistent long-term complaint. Multiple reviewers report that features which appear to be part of the plan price sometimes require an additional monthly charge. Budget beyond the headline plan price and confirm exactly what's included during your demo.

The Android app rates significantly lower than iOS. The iOS app sits around 4.6/5 while the Android app has been rated closer to 3.3/5. If your technicians use Android phones, test the field app thoroughly before committing — this is a real day-to-day usability gap.

Reporting is a weak point. Independent reviewers rate the reporting module below the rest of the product. Detailed revenue breakdowns by technician, job type, or maintenance-plan performance may require workarounds or exports.

Phone support is gated to premium tiers. The Basic plan is email and chat only — phone support requires higher plans. If you want to call a human when something breaks mid-season, factor the plan level into your decision.

What Real Users Say

HVAC and field service reviewers on Capterra consistently single out the maintenance-plan and recurring-billing automation as the feature that changed their recurring revenue — enrolling customers once and letting the software bill and schedule from then on.

Reviewers running 5+ service calls a day frequently cite the dispatch board and automatic "on my way" texts as justifying the subscription on their own — fewer "where's the tech?" calls to the office.

The most common critical themes across Capterra and independent reviews are add-on charges that weren't obvious upfront, weaker reporting, and the gap between the iOS and Android apps.

How It Compares

Against Jobber: Jobber has a stronger client self-service portal and better Android parity, and it's the better pick for Canadian shops and multi-trade businesses. Housecall Pro wins on maintenance-plan automation and marketing/review tools. See the Jobber vs Housecall Pro comparison.

Against ServiceTitan: ServiceTitan is far more powerful but built for 20+ tech operations, with $5,000–$50,000+ implementation. For most HVAC shops, Housecall Pro delivers the workflow they actually need at a fraction of the cost and complexity. See the Housecall Pro vs ServiceTitan comparison.

Who This Is NOT For

Shops where every technician uses Android. The Android app's weaker ratings are a genuine day-to-day friction. Jobber has better Android parity — test both if your field team is Android-only.

Established shops married to QuickBooks Desktop. Housecall Pro syncs with QuickBooks Online, not the two-way Desktop sync FieldEdge is known for. If your accounting lives on QuickBooks Desktop, see the FieldEdge review.

Large operations (20+ techs) needing advanced dispatch and reporting. At that scale, ServiceTitan's depth may justify its cost. Housecall Pro is built for shops up to roughly 20 technicians.

Contractors who need heavy custom reporting. Reporting is Housecall Pro's weakest area. If board-level analytics drive your decisions, test the reporting module specifically before committing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Housecall Pro good for HVAC businesses?

Yes — for most residential HVAC shops with 3–20 technicians it's one of the two strongest choices (Jobber being the other). Its standout feature for HVAC is Service Plans: it auto-bills and auto-schedules recurring maintenance agreements, which is the core of HVAC recurring revenue. It also handles dispatch, GPS tracking, "on my way" texts, consumer financing for installs, and online booking well. The main catches are add-on cost creep, a weaker Android app, and phone support being limited to premium plans.

Does Housecall Pro handle HVAC maintenance agreements?

Yes, and it's the reason many HVAC shops choose it. The Service Plans feature lets you build a maintenance agreement (for example, two tune-ups a year), enroll customers, and have the software automatically bill them monthly, quarterly, or annually while scheduling each visit and sending reminders. Customers can pay by card, ACH, or digital wallet. It turns a maintenance program from a manual chore into a recurring-revenue engine that largely runs itself. Note that Service Plans require the Essentials plan or above, not Basic.

How much does Housecall Pro cost for an HVAC business?

Basic is about $79/mo monthly (or ~$59/mo annual) for 1 user. Essentials — the practical starting point for HVAC because it includes Service Plans and online booking — is about $189/mo monthly (~$149/mo annual) for up to 5 users. MAX is about $329/mo monthly (~$299/mo annual) for up to 8 users, with additional users around $35/month each. Verify current pricing on housecallpro.com, as plans change.

Does Housecall Pro work in Canada?

Yes — Housecall Pro explicitly covers US and Canadian customers, with the same features across the border. Pricing is in USD; Canadian users pay the CAD equivalent at the prevailing exchange rate. If cross-border coverage matters, Jobber (headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta) is the other tool here with equally strong Canadian support.

Is the Housecall Pro Android app as good as iPhone?

No — this is a documented gap. The iOS app rates around 4.6/5 while the Android app has been rated closer to 3.3/5. The core functions work on both, but Android users report more friction. If your entire field team uses Android, test the app thoroughly during a demo, and compare against Jobber, which has stronger parity between its iOS and Android apps.
