Quick Verdict

FieldEdge's parent company was founded by HVAC contractors in 1980; the current cloud product dates from 2016, and that history shows in two areas newer tools handle less well: two-way QuickBooks Desktop sync and flat-rate pricebooks. If your accounting runs on QuickBooks Desktop and you don't intend to move it, FieldEdge's deep, bidirectional sync is its single strongest selling point — invoices, payments, and customer records flow both ways without double entry. FieldEdge Flat Rate, included on every plan, gives techs a professional pricebook in the field. Its Capterra customer-service score is 4.4/5.

The trade-offs are real: pricing is quote only, with no published prices, there's no free trial, and its overall Capterra rating (4.2/5) is the lowest in this group, with some reviewers citing bugs. For a shop not tied to QuickBooks Desktop, Housecall Pro or Jobber is usually easier and cheaper to run.

What FieldEdge Actually Does

FieldEdge is a full field service management platform aimed squarely at established HVAC, plumbing, and electrical contractors. It covers dispatch, scheduling, mobile work orders, service agreements, invoicing, and customer management. Two capabilities define it against the newer generation of tools:

Two-way QuickBooks Desktop sync. This is FieldEdge's signature. Many established HVAC shops run their books on QuickBooks Desktop and have no desire to migrate to QuickBooks Online. FieldEdge syncs bidirectionally with Desktop — job and invoice data flows from the field into your accounting, and customer and payment data flows back — eliminating the double entry that plagues shops trying to bolt a modern field app onto an older accounting system.

Flat-rate pricebook (FieldEdge Flat Rate). FieldEdge includes a flat-rate pricing library on every plan so technicians can present consistent, professional, itemized pricing in the field rather than quoting off the cuff. For HVAC shops that sell on flat-rate rather than time-and-materials, this standardizes what every tech charges.

It also handles recurring service agreements — important for HVAC maintenance-plan revenue — and provides a technician mobile app for work orders, though reviewers rate the mobile experience below the polish of Housecall Pro and Jobber.

The Catch

Quote-only pricing, no free trial. You can't see a price without a sales demo, and there's no free trial — so you commit before you've fully tested it. Ask whether your quote includes setup or onboarding fees. That's a higher-friction buying process than the card-free trials from Jobber and Housecall Pro.

Lowest overall Capterra score in this group. FieldEdge sits at 4.2/5 (306 reviews) — below Housecall Pro, Jobber, and ServiceTitan. Some reviewers report bugs and crashes that disrupt workflow and take time to resolve, even as they praise the support team.

Multi-week onboarding. FieldEdge isn't a same-week, self-serve setup. Plan for a phased implementation, which matters if you need to be running quickly.

Mobile app rated below the newer tools. The technician app works but doesn't match the polish of Housecall Pro's or Jobber's. If field-app usability is a priority for your techs, weigh this.

What Real Users Say

FieldEdge's most consistent praise is for its customer support — its Capterra support sub-score (4.4/5) is the strongest of any tool in this HVAC group, with reviewers describing help as prompt and effective.

Long-tenured HVAC shops repeatedly cite the QuickBooks Desktop integration as the reason they stay — it solves a specific, real problem that Online-only tools don't.

The recurring criticism is software bugs: some reviewers report crashes and issues that disrupt daily workflow and can take time to resolve, which pulls the overall rating below the newer competitors.

How It Compares

Against Jobber: Jobber is easier to implement, cheaper to start, has a stronger client portal and better mobile apps, and covers Canada natively. FieldEdge wins for shops committed to QuickBooks Desktop and flat-rate pricebooks. See the Jobber vs FieldEdge comparison.

Against ServiceTitan: Both target established shops, but ServiceTitan is heavier, pricier, and built for 20+ techs, while FieldEdge is more accessible for mid-sized HVAC businesses. See the FieldEdge vs ServiceTitan comparison.

Who This Is NOT For

New or small HVAC shops that want to be running this week. The quote-only, demo-first sales process is overkill for a solo operator or 2–3 person shop. Jobber or Housecall Pro (both self-serve, with free trials) is a faster start — see the Jobber review.

Shops that don't use QuickBooks Desktop. FieldEdge's biggest advantage is the Desktop sync. If you run QuickBooks Online or no QuickBooks at all, you're paying for a strength you won't use — the newer tools integrate with QuickBooks Online more simply (Jobber from Connect, Housecall Pro from Essentials).

Maintenance-plan-first businesses wanting the easiest automation. FieldEdge has service agreements (advanced agreements on Premier and Elite), but Housecall Pro's Service Plans (MAX plan or paid add-on) are the smoother auto-bill/auto-schedule experience. See the Housecall Pro review.

Canadian HVAC businesses. FieldEdge is US-focused; Canadian shops are better served by Jobber (headquartered in Canada) or Housecall Pro. Confirm coverage before committing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does FieldEdge cost?

FieldEdge is quote-only — it doesn't publish public pricing. It sells three plans — Select, Premier, and Elite — priced by quote. Warehouse inventory management is on Elite only, and only for shops on QuickBooks Desktop Enterprise with Advanced Inventory. Ask for any setup fee in writing. There's no free trial — you request a demo and a custom quote. Confirm your exact numbers with FieldEdge, as pricing depends on team size and plan tier.

Is FieldEdge good for HVAC businesses?

It's a strong fit for one specific profile: an established HVAC shop that runs its accounting on QuickBooks Desktop and wants flat-rate pricebooks in the field. FieldEdge's two-way Desktop sync and FieldEdge Flat Rate are genuinely better than what Jobber or Housecall Pro offer for those needs, and its Capterra customer-service score is 4.4/5. For newer, smaller, Canadian, or maintenance-plan-first shops, the newer tools are usually easier and cheaper.

Does FieldEdge integrate with QuickBooks?

Yes — and this is its standout strength. FieldEdge offers two-way sync with QuickBooks Desktop, which most modern field service tools don't. Job, invoice, customer, and payment data flows bidirectionally, eliminating double entry for shops that keep their books on Desktop. If you use QuickBooks Online instead, Jobber and Housecall Pro both integrate with it more simply, which narrows FieldEdge's advantage considerably.

FieldEdge vs Housecall Pro — which is better for HVAC?

FieldEdge if you're committed to QuickBooks Desktop and flat-rate pricebooks and you're an established shop. Housecall Pro if you want easier setup, a free-to-explore self-serve experience, a dedicated maintenance-plan module (Service Plans, MAX plan or paid add-on), and a 14-day free trial. Housecall Pro integrates with QuickBooks Online (from Essentials) rather than Desktop. For most residential HVAC shops without a QuickBooks Desktop dependency, Housecall Pro is the simpler, faster choice.
