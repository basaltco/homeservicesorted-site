HVAC Software & EPA 608 Refrigerant Tracking: The 2026 Rules

Most HVAC software marketing waves at "compliance" without saying what your software actually does for EPA Section 608. This guide is the honest version: what the recordkeeping rules require, what changed in January 2026, and exactly where field service tools like Jobber and Housecall Pro help — and where they don't. If you're choosing software partly to stay on the right side of the EPA, read this before you assume the software solves it.

This is a general guide as of July 15, 2026, not legal or regulatory advice. EPA rules change and enforcement varies. Confirm your obligations with the EPA, a compliance professional, or your state/provincial regulator before relying on any of this.

What Changed in 2026 (Read This First)

The AIM Act expansion lowered the HFC recordkeeping threshold from 50 pounds to 15 pounds, effective January 2026. In practice, that means most HVAC shops now manage roughly 3–5× more regulated equipment than before — units that used to fall below the paperwork threshold now trigger leak-inspection and recordkeeping duties. Spreadsheet-and-shoebox tracking that worked at the 50-lb threshold breaks down fast at 15 lb.

The stakes are real: the maximum civil penalty under the Clean Air Act for refrigerant violations is $124,426 per day, per violation (as of January 2025). This isn't a box-ticking exercise — it's the single strongest reason to get your refrigerant records out of paper and into a system.

What EPA Section 608 Actually Requires

Section 608 of the Clean Air Act (40 CFR Part 82, Subpart F) governs how technicians handle refrigerants. The core obligations that touch your daily paperwork:

RequirementWhat it means for your shop
Technician certificationAnyone who maintains, services, repairs, or disposes of equipment that could release refrigerant must be EPA 608-certified. Keep a copy of each tech's certification at your place of business.
Recordkeeping retentionKeep required records for at least three years, accessible at the appliance location or your business address.
Service invoices (larger appliances)When servicing an appliance containing 50+ lb of refrigerant, the invoice to the owner must state the amount of refrigerant added.
Leak inspection & repair verificationKeep records of leak inspections and of tests verifying repairs on appliances containing 50+ lb of refrigerant.
Disposal recordsWhen disposing of appliances containing between 5 and 50 lb of refrigerant, keep records of the disposal.
HFC threshold (2026)The AIM Act expansion pulls more HFC equipment into scope by lowering the recordkeeping threshold to 15 lb — expanding how much of your work now generates records.

Simplified summary of key duties — not the full regulation. See the EPA Section 608 recordkeeping page for authoritative detail.

What Your Software Can Do

General field service platforms — Jobber, Housecall Pro, FieldEdge, ServiceTitan — are genuinely useful for the recordkeeping side of Section 608, and far better than paper or a shared spreadsheet:

  • A per-job record of refrigerant added or recovered. Log the amount on the work order so it's captured against the specific appliance and customer, and can appear on the invoice.
  • Attachments and photos. Attach leak-inspection notes, repair-verification results, and photos to the job — creating a timestamped, searchable evidence trail.
  • Complete service history per customer/unit. When an inspector or a customer asks what was done to a unit and when, it's one lookup instead of digging through folders.
  • Retention by default. Cloud records don't get lost in a truck or a filing cabinet, which helps with the three-year retention duty.
  • Certification storage. Keep copies of technician 608 certifications attached to staff records so they're always accessible at your place of business.

What Your Software Can't Do (Be Honest With Yourself)

None of the mainstream HVAC field service tools are dedicated refrigerant-compliance systems. Specifically:

  • They don't auto-file EPA reports. Storing records is not the same as filing whatever reporting your situation requires. That remains your responsibility.
  • They're not a regulated cylinder-inventory ledger. Tracking refrigerant purchased, on-truck, used, and recovered at the cylinder level — the kind of reconciliation a serious compliance program wants — is not what these tools are built to do out of the box.
  • They don't guarantee you captured the right fields. The software will store what your tech enters; it won't know that a 50+ lb appliance needed the refrigerant amount on the invoice unless your process makes the tech record it.
  • They don't replace certification or training. Software doesn't make an uncertified tech compliant.

The practical takeaway: field service software makes the records defensible and retrievable, which is a real compliance benefit — but the process (what gets recorded, when, and how it's reported) is still on you. For shops with heavy refrigerant volume, a dedicated refrigerant-tracking add-on or a purpose-built compliance system may be worth layering on top.

A Practical Setup That Works

You don't need enterprise software to build a defensible refrigerant record. A workable approach in Jobber or Housecall Pro:

  1. Create a standard job checklist that prompts the tech to record refrigerant type and amount added/recovered on every applicable job.
  2. Require a photo of the data plate and any leak-inspection result attached to the work order.
  3. Put the refrigerant amount on the invoice for 50+ lb appliances — both tools let you add line items or notes that carry to the invoice.
  4. Store tech 608 certifications as attachments on each employee's record.
  5. Rely on the cloud history for the three-year retention requirement, and export periodically as a backup.

This turns your everyday job records into a searchable audit trail — which, with the 15-lb threshold now pulling far more equipment into scope, is exactly the shift most shops need to make in 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does HVAC software keep me EPA 608 compliant?

Partly. Field service software like Jobber and Housecall Pro helps you meet the recordkeeping side of Section 608 — logging refrigerant added or recovered per job, attaching leak-inspection notes and photos, keeping service history, and retaining records — which is far better than paper. But it doesn't make you compliant on its own: it won't auto-file EPA reports, act as a regulated cylinder-inventory ledger, or ensure your techs capture the right fields. Compliance is a process; software supports the records, not the whole obligation.

What changed with refrigerant recordkeeping in 2026?

The AIM Act expansion lowered the HFC recordkeeping threshold from 50 pounds to 15 pounds, effective January 2026. Equipment that previously fell below the paperwork threshold now triggers leak-inspection and recordkeeping duties, so most HVAC shops are managing roughly 3–5× more regulated equipment. Combined with civil penalties up to $124,426 per day per violation, that's the main reason shops are moving refrigerant records off spreadsheets and into software this year.

Do I have to put refrigerant amounts on the invoice?

For appliances containing 50 or more pounds of refrigerant, yes — when you service them, the invoice to the owner must state the amount of refrigerant added. Jobber and Housecall Pro both let you add this as a line item or note that carries onto the invoice, so building it into your standard job process is straightforward. Confirm the current specifics with the EPA, as thresholds and details are exactly what the 2026 changes affected.

How long do I need to keep refrigerant records?

At least three years, kept accessible at either the appliance location or your business address. Cloud-based field service software helps here because records are retained automatically rather than living in a filing cabinet or a truck. As a backup, export your job and refrigerant records periodically so you're not solely dependent on one system. This is general guidance — verify retention specifics for your situation with the EPA or a compliance professional.

Is there dedicated refrigerant tracking software?

Yes — there are purpose-built refrigerant-compliance and cylinder-tracking systems that go beyond what general field service tools do, handling cylinder-level inventory reconciliation and structured reporting. For shops with heavy refrigerant volume or strict internal compliance programs, layering one of those on top of Jobber or Housecall Pro can make sense. For most small-to-mid HVAC shops, disciplined use of a general tool's job records, attachments, and history is a large step up from paper and covers the core recordkeeping duties.
