FieldEdge vs ServiceTitan for HVAC (2026)

FieldEdge and ServiceTitan are the two "serious operations" platforms in HVAC — both quote-only, both aimed at established shops rather than solo operators, and both pricier and heavier than Jobber or Housecall Pro. If you're comparing these two specifically, you're likely a growing HVAC business weighing a deeper platform. The honest headline: they suit different sizes, and many shops looking at them would actually be better served by neither. We have no affiliate relationship with either FieldEdge or ServiceTitan, so this comparison recommends whatever genuinely fits.

The Short Answer

FieldEdge is the better fit for an established mid-sized HVAC shop that runs QuickBooks Desktop and wants flat-rate pricebooks — its two-way Desktop sync is its defining strength, and it's more accessible and cheaper to run than ServiceTitan. ServiceTitan is the better fit only for larger operations (roughly 20+ technicians) with dedicated admin staff and the budget for a $5,000–$50,000+ implementation — it's more powerful, but the vendor itself says it isn't optimized for companies with 3 or fewer techs.

If you're a small-to-mid HVAC shop (3–20 techs) without a QuickBooks Desktop dependency, the more useful comparison is probably not this one — start with Housecall Pro or Jobber instead.

Where FieldEdge Wins

QuickBooks Desktop shops and lower cost of entry. FieldEdge's two-way QuickBooks Desktop sync is the reason established shops choose it, and while both tools cost real money, FieldEdge's setup fee ($500–$2,000) and multi-week onboarding are a fraction of ServiceTitan's five-figure, multi-month implementation. For a mid-sized shop that wants more depth than Jobber or Housecall Pro without committing to an enterprise rollout, FieldEdge is the more accessible step up. Its customer support is also well-rated (4.4/5 Capterra sub-score).

Where ServiceTitan Wins

Scale, dispatch depth, and marketing analytics. At 20+ technicians, ServiceTitan's advanced dispatch, capacity planning, campaign attribution, and enterprise reporting are in a different league. Larger operations credit it with meaningful growth. If you have the size and admin capacity to use that depth, ServiceTitan does things FieldEdge simply doesn't reach — the trade-off is cost and a demanding implementation.

The Honest Caveat

Most shops comparing these two are smaller than they think they need to be. ServiceTitan states it isn't optimized for companies with 3 or fewer techs, and its cost/complexity rarely pays off below ~20. FieldEdge's advantage is narrowly about QuickBooks Desktop and flat-rate pricing. If neither of those specific needs applies to you, a 3–20 tech HVAC shop will usually get more value, faster and cheaper, from Housecall Pro (maintenance plans) or Jobber (all-round + Canada). Don't buy enterprise complexity you won't use.

Which Should You Pick?

Pick FieldEdge if: you're an established mid-sized shop on QuickBooks Desktop, you want flat-rate pricebooks, and you want more depth than the newer tools without an enterprise rollout.

Pick ServiceTitan if: you have 20+ technicians, dedicated admin staff, and the budget for a five-figure implementation, and you need best-in-class dispatch, reporting, and marketing analytics.

Pick neither if: you're a 3–20 tech shop without a QuickBooks Desktop dependency — Housecall Pro or Jobber will serve you better for less.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is FieldEdge or ServiceTitan better for HVAC?

It depends on size. FieldEdge suits established mid-sized shops (roughly 5–50 techs), especially those on QuickBooks Desktop who want flat-rate pricebooks — it's cheaper to implement and faster to roll out. ServiceTitan is more powerful but built for larger operations (20+ techs) with dedicated admin staff and a budget for $5,000–$50,000+ implementation. For most small-to-mid HVAC shops without a QuickBooks Desktop dependency, neither is the best value — Housecall Pro or Jobber usually is.

Which is more expensive, FieldEdge or ServiceTitan?

ServiceTitan, by a wide margin. FieldEdge runs roughly $100 per office user and $125 per tech per month, with a one-time setup of $500–$2,000. ServiceTitan runs roughly $245–$500 per technician per month plus a $5,000–$50,000+ implementation and a 6–12 month rollout. A 10-tech shop can spend $50,000–$70,000+ with ServiceTitan in year one. Both are quote-only, so confirm exact numbers directly.

Do both integrate with QuickBooks Desktop?

Both work with QuickBooks, but FieldEdge's two-way QuickBooks Desktop sync is its signature strength and a common reason shops choose it over newer tools. ServiceTitan also integrates with QuickBooks. If keeping your books on QuickBooks Desktop with tight bidirectional sync is a priority and you don't need ServiceTitan's enterprise scale, FieldEdge is the more targeted — and much cheaper — choice.
