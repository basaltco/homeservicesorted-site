Best HVAC Business Software (2026)

An HVAC business lives or dies on two things software touches directly: keeping the dispatch board full in season, and turning one-time service calls into recurring maintenance-plan revenue. Get the second one right and you build a book of maintenance agreements that pays you every spring and fall whether the phone rings or not. The wrong software makes both harder than they need to be.

The hard part isn't finding field service software — it's knowing which tool actually fits an HVAC contractor with 3–20 technicians, versus one built for a 50-truck operation with a full-time dispatcher, or one so generic that maintenance agreements and refrigerant records are an afterthought. This guide covers the four tools US and Canadian HVAC businesses evaluate most — Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge, and ServiceTitan — based on verified pricing and reviews. No sponsored content. Where one tool fits a specific size or type of HVAC business better than another, we say so directly.

Quick Verdict

Pick Housecall Pro if recurring maintenance plans are central to your business — its Service Plans feature auto-bills and auto-schedules tune-up agreements (e.g. two visits a year), and no other tool here makes that workflow as easy. It's the strongest all-round fit for a residential HVAC shop with 3–20 techs. Pick Jobber if you want the same job-to-cash workflow with a stronger client self-service portal, better Android apps, and native US + Canada coverage — the best choice for Canadian HVAC shops and multi-trade businesses. Pick FieldEdge if you run QuickBooks Desktop and want deep two-way accounting sync plus flat-rate pricebooks — its 45-year HVAC heritage shows in those areas, though pricing is quote-only and setup is slower. Pick ServiceTitan only if you're a 20+ technician operation with a dedicated admin and the budget for enterprise software — the vendor itself says it isn't optimized for companies with 3 or fewer techs, and implementation runs $5,000–$50,000+.

If you're not sure, start with Housecall Pro or Jobber — both are built for HVAC shops your size, and Jobber's 14-day free trial needs no card.

Why This Decision Matters More Than the Monthly Cost

For an HVAC business, choosing wrong is expensive in a way the subscription price doesn't show. The first hidden cost is switching: contractors consistently report that migrating platforms — exporting job history, rebuilding pricebooks, re-entering maintenance agreements, retraining techs — takes days to weeks, usually landing right when the season is busiest. The second is the enterprise trap: signing up for ServiceTitan as a 5-truck shop, then spending $50,000 and six months on an implementation built for operations four times your size, and never extracting the value.

But the biggest one is invisible: your maintenance-agreement book is the most valuable asset your software touches. A residential customer on a twice-yearly tune-up plan is worth far more over five years than the one-time repair that won you them. If your software makes those agreements easy to sell, auto-bills them, and auto-schedules the spring and fall visits, that recurring revenue compounds. If it makes them a manual chore, you sell fewer, forget to schedule some, and lose the renewals — quietly, one lapsed plan at a time. That is why the maintenance-plan workflow, not the sticker price, should drive this decision.

For Canadian HVAC businesses, Jobber and Housecall Pro both serve US and Canadian customers equally — pricing is in USD, features are identical across the border, and Jobber's Edmonton, Alberta headquarters gives it especially strong Canadian penetration. FieldEdge and ServiceTitan are more US-focused; Canadian contractors should confirm coverage and payment processing before committing.

The Four Tools Worth Your Time

Best for maintenance plans

Housecall Pro — from $79/mo

Housecall Pro is arguably the strongest all-round fit for a residential HVAC shop, and the reason is its Service Plans (maintenance agreement) feature. You set up a plan once — say two tune-ups a year at $199/year — and Housecall Pro handles the recurring billing, the scheduling reminders, and the customer communication automatically. For a business trying to build a book of recurring maintenance revenue, that automation is the whole game. Add the polished online booking page, real-time GPS dispatch, automatic "on my way" texts, and built-in consumer financing, and it covers the HVAC workflow cleanly. It's also the most-reviewed tool here — 4.7/5 on Capterra from over 2,700 customers.

Catch: Cost creep from add-ons is the most consistent long-term complaint — features that look included sometimes carry an extra monthly charge. The reporting module is rated weak by independent reviewers. The Android app rates significantly lower than iOS (approximately 3.3 vs 4.6) — a real issue if your techs run Android. Phone support is locked to premium tiers; the Basic plan is email and chat only.

Best all-rounder / best for Canada

Jobber — from $49/mo

Jobber is the strongest all-rounder and the clearest choice for a growing HVAC business or any Canadian shop. Scheduling, quoting, dispatching, invoicing, and payment collection all work cleanly, and the client self-service portal — where customers approve quotes, track jobs, view service history, and pay online — is the best in this group at the price. Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, so it works identically for US and Canadian HVAC businesses with no geographic workarounds. It also has genuinely good mobile apps on both iOS and Android, and if you run HVAC alongside plumbing or electrical, the same account covers all of it.

Catch: Per-user pricing jumps sharply — Core ($49/mo) covers 1 user; adding a second means Connect at $119/mo. Recurring maintenance agreements can be set up as recurring jobs, but Jobber doesn't auto-bill them the way Housecall Pro's Service Plans do — for a maintenance-plan-heavy HVAC shop, that's a real gap. Online booking requires the Connect plan; reporting granularity is less than some expect.

Best for QuickBooks Desktop shops

FieldEdge — quote only (~$100/office user + $125/tech)

FieldEdge has 45 years of HVAC heritage, and it shows in two areas the newer tools handle less well: two-way QuickBooks Desktop sync and flat-rate pricebooks. If your accounting runs on QuickBooks Desktop and you don't want to move it, FieldEdge's deep sync is its standout feature — invoices, payments, and customer records flow both ways without double entry. The Coolfront flat-rate integration gives your techs a professional pricebook in the field. It handles dispatch, service agreements, and mobile work orders competently for established HVAC shops in the 5–50 tech range. Its Capterra support sub-score (4.4/5) is the best of any tool here.

Catch: Pricing is quote-only and not cheap — roughly $100/office user plus $125/tech per month, with $500–$2,000 setup and a multi-week onboarding. A typical 5-tech shop pays $380–$450/mo once common add-ons (inventory, advanced reporting) are included. Overall Capterra rating (4.2/5) is the lowest in this group, with some reviewers citing bugs that disrupt workflow. No free trial — demo only.

If you're under about 20 technicians, start elsewhere and revisit ServiceTitan only when scale and a dedicated admin team make the cost defensible.

What These Tools Don't Do

None of these platforms replace payroll or accounting software — you'll still need QuickBooks, Gusto, or ADP for paying technicians and filing taxes (FieldEdge and ServiceTitan sync with QuickBooks; Jobber and Housecall Pro integrate with QuickBooks Online). And none of them are dedicated refrigerant-compliance systems. They store job records, attachments, and service history — which is far better than paper for the recordkeeping EPA Section 608 requires — but they do not track refrigerant cylinder inventory as a regulated ledger or auto-file EPA reports. If you're evaluating software partly to solve compliance, read the EPA 608 and refrigerant tracking guide first so you know exactly where software helps and where it doesn't.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best HVAC software for a small business?

For most HVAC shops with 3–20 technicians, Housecall Pro or Jobber is the right answer. Housecall Pro wins if recurring maintenance plans are central to your revenue — its Service Plans feature auto-bills and auto-schedules tune-up agreements better than anything else here. Jobber wins if you want the strongest client self-service portal, the best Android apps, and native US + Canada coverage. Both cost a fraction of the enterprise tools and can be running in days. FieldEdge suits established shops tied to QuickBooks Desktop; ServiceTitan is enterprise software the vendor itself says isn't optimized for companies with 3 or fewer techs.

Is ServiceTitan worth it for a small HVAC company?

Usually not, if "small" means under about 20 technicians. ServiceTitan is powerful — best-in-class dispatch, reporting, and marketing — but it's priced and built for larger operations: roughly $245–$500 per technician per month, $5,000–$50,000+ implementation, and a six-to-twelve-month rollout. ServiceTitan itself states the platform is "not optimized for companies with 3 or fewer technicians." For a small HVAC shop, Housecall Pro or Jobber delivers the workflow you actually need at a fraction of the cost and complexity, and you can be live in days. Revisit ServiceTitan when you have the truck count, admin staff, and budget to justify it.

Which HVAC software is best for maintenance agreements?

Housecall Pro. Its Service Plans (also called maintenance plans) feature is built specifically for the HVAC recurring-revenue model: you set up a plan such as two tune-ups a year, and the software automatically bills the customer on the schedule you choose (monthly, quarterly, or annually), schedules the visits, and sends the reminders. Customers can pay by card, ACH, or digital wallet. Jobber can handle maintenance work as recurring jobs but doesn't auto-bill agreements the same way, so you do more of the billing manually. FieldEdge and ServiceTitan both have service-agreement modules but cost more and take longer to set up.

Does HVAC software help with EPA 608 refrigerant compliance?

Partly, and it's important to be clear about the limits. General field service tools like Jobber and Housecall Pro let you record refrigerant added on a job, attach leak-inspection notes and photos, and keep a searchable service history — which is a genuine improvement over paper or spreadsheets for the recordkeeping EPA Section 608 requires. But none of them are dedicated refrigerant-tracking ledgers, and none auto-file EPA reports. With the January 2026 AIM Act expansion lowering the HFC recordkeeping threshold from 50 to 15 pounds, more of your equipment is now regulated. Read our EPA 608 guide for exactly what software can and can't do for compliance.

Which HVAC software works in Canada?

Jobber and Housecall Pro both work identically in the US and Canada, with no geo-restrictions or feature differences across the border. Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, and has particularly strong Canadian market penetration — many Canadian HVAC businesses use it as their primary platform. Housecall Pro explicitly covers US and Canadian customers. FieldEdge and ServiceTitan are more US-focused; Canadian contractors should confirm coverage, payment processing, and support before committing. Pricing across all platforms is in USD; Canadian users pay the CAD equivalent at the prevailing exchange rate.
