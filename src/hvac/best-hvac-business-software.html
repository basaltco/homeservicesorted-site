Best HVAC Business Software (2026)

An HVAC business lives or dies on two things software touches directly: keeping the dispatch board full in season, and turning one-time service calls into recurring maintenance-plan revenue. Get the second one right and you build a book of maintenance agreements that pays you every spring and fall whether the phone rings or not. The wrong software makes both harder than they need to be.

The hard part isn't finding field service software — it's knowing which tool actually fits an HVAC contractor with 3–20 technicians, versus one built for a 50-truck operation with a full-time dispatcher, or one so generic that maintenance agreements and refrigerant records are an afterthought. This guide covers four widely used tools for US and Canadian HVAC businesses — Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge, and ServiceTitan — based on each vendor's published plans and prices, app-store ratings, and Capterra scores, checked November 2026. Quick Verdict

Pick Housecall Pro if you want a dedicated maintenance-agreement module. Its Service Plans handle the agreement itself — recurring visits, renewals, and automatic billing — but they come with the MAX plan ($329/mo, or $299/mo billed annually, for 8 users) or as a paid add-on on Basic and Essentials, and we couldn't find a published add-on price. It also has the higher-rated Android app of the two (4.6/5 on Google Play vs Jobber's 4.4/5) and includes live phone and chat support on every plan. Pick Jobber if recurring jobs with automatic payments are enough: from the Connect plan, Jobber automatically charges a client's saved payment method on a recurring job's billing schedule. Add a strong client self-service portal, pricing published by team size, and Edmonton headquarters, and it's an easy fit for multi-trade and Canadian shops. Pick FieldEdge if you run QuickBooks Desktop and want flat-rate pricing in the field — it has HVAC roots going back to 1980, but pricing is quote only. Pick ServiceTitan only if you're a 20+ technician operation with a dedicated admin and the budget for enterprise software. It's quote only, per technician, and in a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians.

If you're not sure, start with Housecall Pro or Jobber — both are built for HVAC shops your size, and both offer a 14-day free trial with no card required.

Why This Decision Matters More Than the Monthly Cost

For an HVAC business, choosing wrong is expensive in a way the subscription price doesn't show. The first hidden cost is switching: migrating platforms — exporting job history, rebuilding pricebooks, re-entering maintenance agreements, retraining techs — takes real time, and it usually lands right when the season is busiest. The second is the enterprise trap: signing up for ServiceTitan as a 5-truck shop, then paying for an implementation built for operations four times your size, and never extracting the value.

But the biggest one is invisible: your maintenance-agreement book is the most valuable asset your software touches. A residential customer on a twice-yearly tune-up plan is worth far more over five years than the one-time repair that won you them. If your software makes those agreements easy to sell, auto-bills them, and auto-schedules the spring and fall visits, that recurring revenue compounds. If it makes them a manual chore, you sell fewer, forget to schedule some, and lose the renewals — quietly, one lapsed plan at a time. That is why the maintenance-plan workflow, not the sticker price, should drive this decision.

For Canadian HVAC businesses, Jobber and Housecall Pro both serve US and Canadian customers and list their prices in USD, and Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta. Features aren't identical across the border — Jobber's bank (ACH) payments are US-only, for example — so check payment options before you sign up. FieldEdge and ServiceTitan are more US-focused; Canadian contractors should confirm coverage and payment processing before committing.

The Four Tools Worth Your Time

Maintenance-plan module

Housecall Pro — from $79/mo

Housecall Pro's standout for HVAC is Service Plans, a dedicated maintenance-agreement module. You set up a plan once — say two tune-ups a year at $199/year — and Housecall Pro handles the recurring billing, the scheduling reminders, and the customer communication automatically. For a business trying to build a book of recurring maintenance revenue, that automation is the whole game. Service Plans come with the MAX plan ($329/mo, or $299/mo billed annually, for 8 users) or as a paid add-on on Basic and Essentials; we couldn't find a published add-on price, so ask before you sign up. Add the polished online booking page, GPS tracking (Essentials and up), automatic "on my way" texts, and built-in consumer financing, and it covers the HVAC workflow cleanly. It's also the most-reviewed tool here — 4.7/5 on Capterra from over 2,700 reviews — its Android app rates 4.6/5 on Google Play, and every plan includes live phone and chat support.

Catch: The plan you need costs more than the headline price. Service Plans need MAX or a paid add-on, and flat-rate pricing, QuickBooks Online sync, and GPS tracking start on Essentials ($189/mo). Price the plan that includes the features you'll actually use, not Basic.

Strong all-rounder

Jobber — from $49/mo

Jobber is a strong all-rounder here for a growing HVAC business. Scheduling, quoting, dispatching, invoicing, and payment collection all work cleanly, and the client self-service portal — where customers approve quotes, track jobs, view service history, and pay online — is strong. Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, and serves both US and Canadian HVAC businesses (its bank payments are US-only). Its mobile apps rate 4.8/5 on the App Store and 4.4/5 on Google Play, and if you run HVAC alongside plumbing or electrical, the same account covers all of it.

Catch: Pricing scales with team size. Core is $49/mo for one user, and Jobber's pricing page says you can add users for $29/mo each. Connect — the plan that adds automatic payments and QuickBooks Online sync — is $139/mo for one user or $199/mo for five, month to month, and there's a Plus plan above Grow for larger teams. Jobber can automatically charge a client's saved payment method on a recurring job's billing schedule, but it doesn't show a dedicated maintenance-agreement module — plan catalog, online renewals, member perks — like Housecall Pro's Service Plans.

Best for QuickBooks Desktop shops

FieldEdge — quote only

FieldEdge's parent company was founded by HVAC contractors in 1980; the current cloud product dates from 2016, and that history shows in two areas the newer tools handle less well: two-way QuickBooks Desktop sync and flat-rate pricebooks. If your accounting runs on QuickBooks Desktop and you don't want to move it, FieldEdge's deep sync is its standout feature — invoices, payments, and customer records flow both ways without double entry. FieldEdge Flat Rate, included on every plan, gives your techs a professional pricebook in the field. It handles dispatch, service agreements (basic on Select, advanced on Premier and Elite), and mobile work orders competently for established HVAC shops in the 5–50 tech range. Its Capterra customer-service score is 4.4/5.

Catch: Pricing is quote only — FieldEdge publishes three plans (Select, Premier, Elite) but no prices, so get a written quote that includes any setup fee. Warehouse inventory management is on the Elite plan only, and only for shops on QuickBooks Desktop Enterprise with Advanced Inventory. Its overall Capterra rating (4.2/5 from 314 reviews) is the lowest in this group. No free trial — demo only.

Enterprise only — most readers should skip

ServiceTitan — quote only, per technician

ServiceTitan is built for large operations, with dispatch, reporting, and marketing tools aimed at shops running 20 or more trucks. But it is enterprise software with enterprise economics. It doesn't publish prices: it sells three plans (Starter, Essentials, The Works) on per-technician pricing, by quote only, so ask for the implementation fee and timeline in writing before you sign. In a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians.

Catch: For the typical HVAC shop this guide is written for — 3–20 techs — ServiceTitan is usually the wrong tool: too expensive, too complex, and too slow to implement to justify against Housecall Pro or Jobber. We include it so you can rule it in or out honestly. If you're under about 20 technicians, start elsewhere and revisit ServiceTitan only when scale and a dedicated admin team make the cost defensible.

What These Tools Don't Do

None of these platforms replace payroll or accounting software — you'll still need QuickBooks, Gusto, or ADP for paying technicians and filing taxes (FieldEdge syncs with QuickBooks Online and Desktop; Jobber syncs with QuickBooks Online and Xero from its Connect plan; Housecall Pro syncs with QuickBooks Online from Essentials; ask ServiceTitan which versions it supports). And none of them are dedicated refrigerant-compliance systems. They store job records, attachments, and service history — which is far better than paper for the recordkeeping EPA Section 608 requires — but they do not track refrigerant cylinder inventory as a regulated ledger or auto-file EPA reports. If you're evaluating software partly to solve compliance, read the EPA 608 and refrigerant tracking guide first so you know exactly where software helps and where it doesn't.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best HVAC software for a small business?

For most HVAC shops with 3–20 technicians, Housecall Pro or Jobber is the right answer. Housecall Pro wins if you want a dedicated maintenance-agreement module — its Service Plans (MAX plan or paid add-on) auto-bill and auto-schedule tune-up agreements — and it has the higher-rated Android app. Jobber wins if recurring jobs with automatic payments are enough and you want a strong client self-service portal. Both offer a 14-day free trial with no card, both serve the US and Canada, and both can be running in days. FieldEdge suits established shops tied to QuickBooks Desktop; ServiceTitan is enterprise software, and in a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said it isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians.

Is ServiceTitan worth it for a small HVAC company?

Usually not, if "small" means under about 20 technicians. ServiceTitan is powerful, but it's priced and built for larger operations: it quotes per technician, doesn't publish prices, and you'll want the implementation fee and timeline in writing before you sign. In a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians. For a small HVAC shop, Housecall Pro or Jobber delivers the workflow you actually need at a fraction of the complexity, and you can be live in days. Revisit ServiceTitan when you have the truck count, admin staff, and budget to justify it.

Which HVAC software is best for maintenance agreements?

Housecall Pro, if you want a dedicated module. Its Service Plans (also called maintenance plans) feature is built for the HVAC recurring-revenue model: you set up a plan such as two tune-ups a year, and the software automatically bills the customer on the schedule you choose (monthly, quarterly, or annually), schedules the visits, and sends the reminders. Service Plans come with the MAX plan or as a paid add-on on Basic and Essentials — we couldn't find a published add-on price. Customers can pay by card or bank payment. Jobber handles maintenance work as recurring jobs and, from its Connect plan, can automatically charge a client's saved payment method on the job's billing schedule — but it doesn't show a dedicated agreement module with plan catalogs, renewals, and member perks. FieldEdge has service agreements (advanced agreements on Premier and Elite), and ServiceTitan offers memberships, which its pricing page appears to list only on The Works plan — confirm with sales. Both are quote only and take longer to set up.

Does HVAC software help with EPA 608 refrigerant compliance?

Partly, and it's important to be clear about the limits. General field service tools like Jobber and Housecall Pro let you record refrigerant added on a job, attach leak-inspection notes and photos, and keep a searchable service history — which is a genuine improvement over paper or spreadsheets for the recordkeeping EPA Section 608 requires. But none of them are dedicated refrigerant-tracking ledgers, and none auto-file EPA reports. From January 2026, EPA's AIM Act rules add leak-repair and recordkeeping duties for HFC appliances holding 15 lb or more of refrigerant. Read our EPA 608 guide for exactly what software can and can't do for compliance.

Which HVAC software works in Canada?

Jobber and Housecall Pro both serve US and Canadian businesses. Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, and Housecall Pro explicitly covers US and Canadian customers. Features aren't identical across the border — Jobber's bank (ACH) payments are US-only, for example — so check payment options before you sign up. Canadian contractors should confirm coverage, payment processing, and support before committing. Jobber and Housecall Pro list prices in USD, and FieldEdge and ServiceTitan are quote only — confirm the billing currency with the vendor before you sign up.
