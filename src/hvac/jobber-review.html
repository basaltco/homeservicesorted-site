Quick Verdict

Jobber is the strongest all-rounder for HVAC businesses and the clearest choice for any Canadian HVAC shop. Scheduling, quoting, dispatch, invoicing, and payments all work cleanly, and the client self-service portal — where customers approve quotes, track jobs, view service history, and pay online — is the best in this group at the price. It has genuinely good mobile apps on both iOS and Android, and if you run HVAC alongside plumbing or electrical, one account covers all of it.

The two catches that matter most for HVAC: the per-user price jump (Core at $49/mo covers 1 user; a second person means Connect at $119/mo), and the fact that Jobber doesn't auto-bill maintenance agreements the way Housecall Pro's Service Plans do. If maintenance-plan automation is central to your revenue, weigh that carefully. For everything else, Jobber is the safest starting point — and the 14-day trial needs no card.

What Jobber Actually Does

Jobber is used by over 200,000 service businesses globally and covers the full job lifecycle: scheduling, quoting, dispatching, time tracking, invoicing, and payment collection. For an HVAC shop, the day-to-day workhorses are the dispatch board, the quoting flow (build and send an estimate for a repair or replacement from the app, convert to a job when approved), and the client portal.

The client self-service portal is what separates Jobber from most competitors at this price point. Customers get their own login to approve quotes, view upcoming appointments, track active jobs, see complete service history, and pay invoices online. For an HVAC business serving property managers, landlords, or commercial accounts who want visibility without phone calls, this is a genuine differentiator — and it's stronger than Housecall Pro's equivalent.

Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta. It works identically for US and Canadian HVAC businesses — no geographic workarounds, no separate Canadian pricing — and has particularly strong penetration among Canadian contractors.

How It Works on a Real HVAC Job

A customer requests an AC repair through your website's online booking widget (Connect plan and above). The job lands on the dispatch board and the assigned tech is notified on the app. A reminder goes to the customer before the visit. On site, the tech records the diagnosis, builds a quote for the repair or a replacement system, and sends it — the customer can approve it from their phone through the portal.

Once approved, the job proceeds, the tech logs parts and any refrigerant used, and the invoice generates automatically. If payment isn't collected within a set window, Jobber sends an automated reminder. Everything — appointment, job status, invoice, payment history — is visible to the customer in their portal, which is exactly the interface commercial and property-management clients prefer for routine status without phoning your office.

The Catch

The price jump from Core to Connect is steep. Going from 1 user ($49/mo) to 2 requires Connect at $119/mo — $70/month more for one additional technician. If you're hiring your first tech, factor this in before you do.

Maintenance agreements aren't auto-billed. You can set up recurring maintenance visits as recurring jobs, but Jobber doesn't automatically bill a maintenance-plan subscription the way Housecall Pro's Service Plans do. For an HVAC shop whose model is built on auto-renewing tune-up agreements, this is a real gap — you'll do more of the billing manually.

Online booking requires the Connect plan. If letting customers book directly from your website is a priority, you need at least Connect ($119/mo); it's not on Core.

Reporting is less detailed than some expect. Multiple G2 reviewers note Jobber's reporting, while useful, lacks the granularity of dedicated tools. Revenue breakdowns by tech, job type, or territory can require exports. Test this if detailed reporting matters to you.

What Real Users Say

Reviewers serving property management and commercial accounts consistently cite the client self-service portal as the feature that reduced inbound calls and gave clients direct visibility into job status and invoicing.

G2 reviewers frequently flag the Core-to-Connect pricing jump as a surprise — the solo plan works at $49/month, but adding a first technician pushes to $119/month, which isn't obvious from the pricing page.

Contractors running mixed-trade operations — HVAC plus plumbing, electrical, or handyman work — repeatedly cite Jobber's multi-trade flexibility as the reason they chose it over HVAC-specific tools, since one account covers everything.

Canadian HVAC reviewers rate Jobber especially highly for being a native Canadian product with local support and no cross-border workarounds.

Who This Is NOT For

Established shops running QuickBooks Desktop. Jobber integrates with QuickBooks Online, not the two-way Desktop sync FieldEdge is built around. If your books live on QuickBooks Desktop, see the FieldEdge review.

Solo operators needing only basic job tracking. Core at $49/mo works, but the features that make Jobber shine — online booking, two-way texts, automated follow-ups — require Connect at $119/mo.

Large enterprises (20+ techs) needing advanced dispatch and reporting. Jobber handles up to 15 users on Grow. Above that, ServiceTitan is the enterprise-grade alternative.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Jobber good for HVAC companies?

Yes — it's the strongest all-rounder for HVAC businesses with 2 or more people, and the best choice for Canadian shops. Dispatch, quoting, a best-in-class client portal, solid iOS and Android apps, and multi-trade flexibility make it the most flexible option here. The main gap for HVAC specifically is maintenance-plan billing: Jobber handles recurring visits as recurring jobs but doesn't auto-bill maintenance-agreement subscriptions the way Housecall Pro's Service Plans do. If maintenance plans are core to your revenue, weigh Housecall Pro against it.

Does Jobber work in Canada?

Yes — Jobber is headquartered in Edmonton, Alberta, and serves US and Canadian HVAC businesses identically. No geographic restrictions, separate Canadian pricing, or feature differences by location. Pricing is in USD; Canadian users pay the CAD equivalent. Jobber has strong Canadian market penetration and is one of the most commonly used field service platforms for Canadian contractors — if you operate in Canada, it's the most natural fit here.

Can Jobber handle HVAC maintenance agreements?

Partly. You can set up recurring maintenance visits (for example, spring and fall tune-ups) as recurring jobs, so the visits get scheduled. What Jobber doesn't do as cleanly is auto-bill a maintenance-plan subscription on its own cadence — that's where Housecall Pro's Service Plans feature is stronger. If your business runs on a large book of auto-renewing maintenance agreements, test both; if maintenance plans are a smaller part of a broader service business, Jobber's recurring jobs may be enough.

How much does Jobber cost for an HVAC business?

Core: $49/mo for 1 user. Connect: $119/mo for up to 5 users. Grow: $199/mo for up to 15 users. Annual billing reduces these by about 20%. The practical entry price for a two-person HVAC shop is $119/mo, since Core covers only 1 user. Compare that against Housecall Pro Essentials ($189/mo monthly for up to 5, and the plan that includes maintenance-plan automation).

What's the difference between Jobber Core and Connect?

Core ($49/mo, 1 user) covers scheduling, job management, invoicing, payments, the client portal, and the mobile app. Connect ($119/mo, up to 5 users) adds online booking (so customers book via your website), two-way texts, job costing, and automated follow-ups. If online booking or a team is important, you need Connect — it's not available on Core.
