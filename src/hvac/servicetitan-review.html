Quick Verdict

ServiceTitan is built for large HVAC operations, typically 20 or more trucks with a full-time dispatcher and admin staff. Its dispatch board, reporting, and marketing analytics go deeper than the small-shop tools, and that depth is what you're paying for. If you have the scale, the admin staff, and the budget, it's worth a demo; if you don't, you'll pay for capacity you won't use.

But it is enterprise software with enterprise economics: ServiceTitan doesn't publish prices — it sells Starter, Essentials, and The Works on per-technician pricing, by quote — so get the implementation fee and timeline in writing before you sign. In a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians. For the typical HVAC shop this site is written for — 3 to 20 techs — it's usually the wrong tool: too expensive, too complex, too slow to implement to justify against Housecall Pro or Jobber. We include it so you can rule it in or out with clear eyes.

What ServiceTitan Actually Does

ServiceTitan is a comprehensive operating platform for home service businesses — HVAC, plumbing, and electrical especially. It goes well beyond scheduling and invoicing into full business management: an advanced dispatch board with capacity planning, deep call-booking and CSR tools, a marketing suite with campaign attribution and ROI tracking, dynamic pricebooks, membership/service-agreement management, payroll integration, inventory, and reporting granular enough to run a multi-department operation.

The dispatch and reporting depth is the real draw. Larger HVAC operations use ServiceTitan to see, in real time, which technicians and marketing campaigns are actually profitable, to balance capacity across install and service divisions, and to manage memberships at a scale where the recurring-revenue engine becomes a major line of business. This is the layer of sophistication that Housecall Pro and Jobber, by design, don't reach.

Put simply: a 10-truck HVAC shop is looking at roughly $30,000–$60,000/year in subscription plus a five-figure implementation. That math works when ServiceTitan's efficiency and revenue gains clearly exceed the cost — which is realistic at scale, and usually isn't for a shop with a handful of techs.

The Catch

Cost is enterprise-level. ServiceTitan quotes per technician and doesn't publish prices or implementation costs. In our assessment, expect it to cost well above Housecall Pro's or Jobber's published plans for the same headcount. The value has to come from scale.

Implementation is long and demanding. ServiceTitan doesn't publish implementation timelines for onboarding, data migration, and training, so ask for one in writing. Small teams without dedicated admin staff consistently report the rollout as overwhelming.

ServiceTitan itself flags the small-shop mismatch. In a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians. Independent reviews echo that smaller teams struggle to extract value proportional to the cost and complexity.

Complexity is a feature and a burden. The depth that makes ServiceTitan powerful for large operations is exactly what makes it heavy for small ones. You pay for — and must administer — capabilities a small shop won't use.

What Real Users Say

Larger HVAC operators are frequently effusive, crediting the dispatch board and reporting with driving their growth. At scale, the sentiment is strongly positive.

Small-team reviewers tell a consistent, different story: cost, complexity, and a painful implementation. The recurring theme is that ServiceTitan is built for enterprise, and smaller shops struggle to extract value — several explicitly recommend Housecall Pro, Jobber, or similar for small teams.

Across both camps, the tool's power is not in question — the disagreement is entirely about fit to company size.

Who This Is NOT For

HVAC shops with fewer than about 20 technicians. This is the core message: below roughly 20 techs, the cost and implementation burden usually outweigh the benefit. In a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians — start with Housecall Pro or Jobber instead.

Anyone who needs to be running quickly or cheaply. A sales-led, quoted implementation is the opposite of a fast, low-risk start. If speed or budget matters, this isn't your tool yet.

Shops without dedicated admin capacity. ServiceTitan rewards operations with staff to configure and run it. Without that, its power goes unused while you pay for it.

Canadian small businesses. ServiceTitan is US-focused; Canadian HVAC shops are better served by Jobber (headquartered in Canada) or Housecall Pro. Confirm coverage before considering it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ServiceTitan worth it for a small HVAC company?

Usually not, if "small" means under about 20 technicians. ServiceTitan is powerful, but it's priced and built for larger operations: it quotes per technician, doesn't publish prices, and implementation is quoted on request. In a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians. For a small HVAC shop, Housecall Pro or Jobber gives you the workflow you actually need at a fraction of the cost and lets you go live in days. Revisit ServiceTitan when your truck count, admin staff, and budget justify it.

How much does ServiceTitan cost per technician?

ServiceTitan is quote only and doesn't publish prices. It sells three plans — Starter, Essentials, and The Works — on per-technician pricing. Ask whether implementation is included, and get both numbers in writing before you sign.

Why do people say ServiceTitan is only for big companies?

Because both the pricing and the design assume scale. The per-technician cost, the five-figure implementation, and the multi-month rollout only pay off when spread across a large operation with dedicated admin staff and enough volume for the advanced dispatch, reporting, and marketing tools to move the needle. In a 2025 response to a BBB complaint, ServiceTitan said its software isn't optimized for businesses with fewer than three technicians, and small-team reviewers consistently report the cost and complexity outweigh the benefit at their size.

What should a small HVAC shop use instead of ServiceTitan?

For most HVAC shops with 3–20 techs, Housecall Pro or Jobber. Housecall Pro is the strongest pick if maintenance plans drive your revenue — its Service Plans feature (MAX plan or paid add-on) auto-bills and auto-schedules agreements. Jobber is a strong all-rounder with a strong client portal. Both offer a 14-day free trial with no card. Both cost a fraction of ServiceTitan and can be running within days rather than months. FieldEdge is worth a look if you're an established shop tied to QuickBooks Desktop.
